About the photographer · CV on file

I follow stories
that stay put.

I grew up above my grandmother's diner in a town the size of a rumor. Every booth had a story and a regular who'd been telling it for forty years. I started stealing pictures of them with a hand-me-down camera, mostly because they were never in any of the family albums.

That's still the whole practice. I spend months — sometimes years — with the people and places worth lingering over: a dying plant town, a diner's night crew, a high-school gymnasium in October. The photographs are the honest footnotes of those long afternoons.

I'm based in Portland now, but most of my work happens somewhere you'd have to drive to. My pictures have run in The New York Times, Harper's, Dwell, and a handful of places that printed me first and asked questions never.
