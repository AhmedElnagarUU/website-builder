The firm · A running balance

Two men, one
desk, 1974.

Edward Harlan opened a two-desk office near the river with a handshake and a sense of clean arithmetic. Fifty years later we still run it like a desk you'd want to sit at.

52 yrs

Edward's original notion was simple, and it's still the whole policy: a client's numbers should make sense to the client. Not to us, not to the IRS — to the person who signs the checks. Everything else — the clean opinions, the quiet tax work, the succession plans nobody wants to think about — is just arithmetic in service of that sentence.

We grew the way Chicago businesses do: slowly, word-of-mouth, one manufacturer and one family restaurant at a time. We've kept the books for refrigerator-repair vans and organ-makers, feed stores and foundries. Each one got the same desk treatment — someone senior, on the actual numbers, with a notepad that doesn't close until the client says the explanation lands.

The firm is now led by the third generation, but the name hasn't changed and neither has the floor. Edward still stops by on Thursdays, mostly to check on the filing. We've stopped pretending he doesn't own the place.
