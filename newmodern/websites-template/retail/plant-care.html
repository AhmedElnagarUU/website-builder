Field notes · From our growing bench

Care that's earned at our own workbench.

These notes are written for this city, this sun, and these particular windows. Where they disagree with the internet, trust the greenhouse — we've killed plants too.

Guide 01 · The basics

6 min

Light is the whole game.

Ninety percent of "why is my plant sad" has one answer: it's farther from a window than it thinks it is. A south window in July is a desert; a north window in January is a cave. Learn where the hotspots of your home actually are — midday, check where the sun lands, and take notes.

Houseplants most of us grow want "bright, indirect light," which means: a few feet from a sunny window, or directly in a window that never gets harsh sun. If the leaves reach and stretch toward the window, they're asking for more. If they burn or wash out, they're asking for less.

Workbench note

Rotate your plant a quarter turn every watering. It won't fix a dark room, but it will keep the plant from leaning its whole life toward one window.

Guide 02 · Watering

5 min

Water less than you think, more than you fear.

The most common plant death is a drowning, delivered lovingly. Stick a finger an inch into the soil: if it's still damp, don't water. Nearly every plant we sell prefers to dry out a little between drinks.

Use room-temperature water, water under the leaves, and let it drain fully. A plant sitting in a saucer of water all week is a plant with a cold wet foot, and cold wet feet lead to root rot — the quiet killer.

Workbench note

Every accession tag you see on our plants lists a real watering interval. It's a guess with education behind it — adjust for your season, not our tag.

Guide 03 · The pot

4 min

The pot is the plant's house. Choose it like you'd choose your own.

Drainage is non-negotiable. If a pot has no hole, it's a vase pretending to be a home. If you fall for a beautiful cachepot with no drainage, use it as an outer sleeve — pot the plant in a nursery pot inside, and empty the saucer.

Material matters: terracotta breathes, which means it dries faster and is forgiving for beginners. Glazed ceramic holds moisture longer and rewards confident watering. Match the pot to your watering habits, not its looks alone — though at our studio, we think we can help with both.

Guide 04 · Austin seasons

7 min

Summer is the real winter here.

In July, an unshaded south window cooks. Move the delicate ones back a foot or add a sheer layer. In winter, days shorten fast — growth slows, water needs drop, and the heater dries the air. Group plants together and they'll raise each other's humidity like good neighbors.

We write our care sheets around the extremes of this city, not the average. If a sheet ever sounds wrong for your apartment's actual reality, it is — the plant cares about your window, not our brochure.

Still unsure? Bring us a leaf.

Snap a photo or tuck a cutting in your pocket and come by. Diagnosis is free, friendly, and usually involves a nice chat about your windowsill.
