Est. 2019 · East Austin

A greenhouse grew up behind the kiln.

We started with a wheel in a backyard and a theory that a pot is only half itself without something alive in it.

In 2017, Sam threw his first bowl that didn't immediately collapse. In 2018, Priya propagated a cutting of her grandmother's monstera so successfully that a shelf in her apartment looked like a small jungle that had begun to issue opinions.

They were friends who kept making each other's problems: Sam made pots that had no plants, Priya grew plants that had no pots. In 2019, they rented a garage on E 6th, bought a secondhand kiln, and opened a shop that was genuinely half greenhouse, half studio — a running handshake between the two obsessions.

Six years later the shop has become a Saturday-morning ritual for the neighborhood. Neither of us planned a "concept." The concept is simply: we grow things we like, throw things we need, and refuse to sell either one as an afterthought.

How we trade

No plastic.

You won't find a single plastic nursery pot here. We compost on-site and reuse every tray until it disintegrates — which, for terracotta lovers, is a theological point.

Small growers.

We buy from two regional nurseries we visit ourselves, plus whatever weird thing a customer's grandmother is willing to share a cutting of. Variety store stock, we don't do.

Honest tags.

Our care notes are written in the shop, for this city, with an actual watering interval. If we haven't grown a plant here at least once, it doesn't wear our tag.
