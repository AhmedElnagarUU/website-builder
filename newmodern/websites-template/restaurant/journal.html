The journal

Undispatched letters from the kitchen. What's in season, what the oven found, and the occasional recipe if the team is feeling generous.

Harvest · September 2026

The lambs come down from the hills this week.

Every September a truck rolls in from the coast range with a half-dozen young lambs pasture-finished by a farmer who's been selling to the restaurant since 2016. It's not cheap, it's not year-round, and it's the single best thing to come off the fire all year.

Tonight's braise uses the shoulder with red wine, porcini, and a square of dark chocolate. The racks go whole over oak. The fat sizzles, the room glows, and for about six weeks the menu has a heartbeat in it you can't fake the rest of the season.

By Elia Moretti · 6 min

Pasta Lessons · August 2026

Why we roll pappardelle, not buy it.

There's flour on everything in this kitchen by 10am, and we wouldn't have it any other way. A batch of pappardelle takes forty minutes and tastes like the difference between bread and a distraction.

The dough is two flours and eggs. The rolling is the patient part. The cutting is done fast with a knife that's older than me. The cooking takes ninety seconds — the drying rack to the boiling pot, no resting, no drama.

By Gina Ferrante · 4 min

From the Cellar · June 2026

A small list, chosen by people who drink it.

We don't have a sommelier with a lanyard. We have Rosa, who drinks everything on the list twice and returns the ones that lied to her. It's twelve reds, six whites, and one weird sparkling we keep because it always starts a conversation.

Everything is under our noses for the check we'd actually pay. You want a wine list the size of a phone book? There's a spreadsheet for that, and it's not ours.

By Rosa Deluca · 3 min
