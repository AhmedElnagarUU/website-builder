Beginning

"If you've got forty seats and one oven, you learn everyone's name by the third visit."

The story is unremarkable and then it isn't. In 2014, Elia Moretti — a Portland baker's son whose grandparents emigrated from the Emilia-Romagna hills — quit a comfortable line-cook job to build a wood-fired oven out of reclaimed brick. He'd been planning it for a decade, sketching seam lines on napkins he still has in a drawer behind the pass.

He found a brick in a demolition lot that had once belonged to a bakery six doors from his grandfather's house in Modena. There is no paperwork to prove this. He will die insisting it's true, and the restaurant's entire theory of attention to detail is basically that belief repeated daily.

The room

The dining room seats forty. The counter seats six, directly in front of the hearth, where the fire is warm enough that regulars keep their jackets on in December and the kitchen team signs your menu if you ate something they're proud of. It's loud, it glows, and the pasta is made at the same table where people eat it.

Cronologia

2014

The oven is lit

Elia fires the reclaimed-brick oven for the first time on a rain-soaked October night. It takes thirds days to reach temperature properly.

2017

The truck farm

A steady relationship with two farms begins — weekly orders written on a clipboard, paid on delivery, no middleman. More than half the produce still comes from the same two farms today.

2021

The counter

Six seats added at the hearth after two years of customers asking "what's right there?" The chef's table is born, and the room claims its personality.

2026

Still lit

Twelve years and roughly eleven thousand fires. The oven has never been off, and the reservation book still keeps half the room for whoever walks in.

What we believe

◆

Fire first

If it can be cooked over live fire, it is. The oven isn't a prop — it's the co-chef, and it decides the menu's tone daily.

◆

Short, honest food

Twelve strong dishes beat thirty mediocre ones. If we can't source it well that week, it's off the menu, no heroics.

◆

The room stays open

Half the seats stay for walk-ins, always. A neighborhood table that no one can book at whim isn't a neighborhood table.
