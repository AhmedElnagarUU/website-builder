Project Overview

A pre-war apartment reimagined for modern living

The Mercer Residence began as a 1920s pre-war apartment in Greenwich Village that had remained largely unchanged for decades. The clients — a young family of four — wanted to preserve the apartment's historical character while creating a home that supported their contemporary lifestyle. The original layout featured a formal foyer, compartmentalized rooms, and a closed kitchen that separated the living spaces from the building's southern exposure.

Our approach opened the plan to create a fluid sequence of living, dining, and kitchen spaces connected by wide arched openings that echo the building's original architectural vocabulary. The material palette draws from the apartment's existing elements — original herringbone oak floors were restored and extended, while plaster moldings were repaired and supplemented with new profiles that seamlessly blend with the historic fabric.

Custom walnut cabinetry throughout the apartment provides both storage and spatial definition, creating intimate zones within the open plan without resorting to conventional walls. The master suite features a freestanding wardrobe system designed to function as an architectural element, mediating between the bedroom and the dressing room while maintaining visual continuity.

Design Approach

Honoring history while embracing the present

The greatest challenge of this project was establishing a dialogue between the apartment's pre-war heritage and the family's contemporary needs. Rather than imposing a modern aesthetic onto a historic shell, we sought to understand the original architect's intent and extend it forward in time.

Every intervention was guided by a principle we call "respectful evolution" — new elements should feel as though they could have been part of the original design, but updated for how we live today. This meant studying the proportions of existing moldings, the rhythm of original window placements, and the hierarchy of public to private spaces that characterized pre-war apartment design.

The result is a home that feels simultaneously timeless and thoroughly modern — a space where the family's children will grow up with an appreciation for craftsmanship and architectural integrity, while enjoying every contemporary convenience.
